# Data archive — frozen features & raw captures

The `data/` directory is **gitignored** (raw pcaps, processed feature matrices,
and model checkpoints are not in the repo). To reproduce the paper's tables and
figures you download a published data archive and unpack it into `data/`.

> **The committed `data/results/` is the canonical, frozen paper output.** It is
> the one thing that is *not* regenerated by reproduction: `scripts/reproduce.sh`
> writes to a sandbox (`data/results_demo/`) and compares against it, and a full
> re-run writes to a fresh copy (`A2A_RESULTS_DIR=...`). Nothing in the normal
> workflow overwrites `data/results/`.

## Known limitation: the end of each capture (collections before commit `f38a2828`)

Until commit `f38a2828` (Nov 9, 2026) `capture/recorder.py` ran tcpdump without
`--immediate-mode`. macOS hands captured packets to tcpdump in batches, and the packets still in
the kernel when a capture was stopped were never written. Every collection made through the
recorder before that commit has this loss: C1, the TLS control and the earlier sets collected with
`collect_session.py`, `collect_tls_session.py`, `collect_deployment.py`, `collect_traces.py` and
`collect_wan.py`. The released captures are unchanged; this section documents them. Captures made
by other tools (`frameworks/`, the off-the-shelf systems, `collect_background.py`) are not covered
by this check.

**Measured** (`scripts/check_capture_tail.py` → `data/results/c1_capture_tail.{json,md}`): 12
C1-style traces re-collected with the old recorder while a second capture in immediate mode
recorded the same traffic (`data/raw_c1_tailcheck/`, not part of a release):

- 2 to 5 packets lost per trace (median 0.7% of its packets): the last packets before the stop,
  which are the connections' closes (FIN and ACK). No application data, except one 24-byte record
  in one trace (likely a TLS close alert). The SSE streams are complete.
- Paper 1's 35 per-connection features: the 16 of 36 connections that lost packets change. Counts
  and sizes change by at most 3% (26 of 35 features stay within 5% everywhere). Time-normalised
  features change more, because a recorded connection ends at its last written packet rather
  than at its close: `duration_s`, `mean_iat` and `mean_ibg` by up to 33%, and the cumulative
  bytes at time quantiles q5 to q8 by up to 94% in a few connections. `p25_sz_in`, `p75_sz_in`,
  `iqr_size_in` and `cumul_bytes_q0`–`q4` never change.
- The 195 trace features: 127 stay within 5% in every trace; the largest changes are in features
  that the connections' ends define (`sys.flow_end_spread`, and the spread across flows of counts
  and durations).

Every analysis of these collections computes all features the same way from the recorded
captures, so the features describe each connection up to its last written packet. First-request
sizes (paper 1's workflow result) and the connection graph (topology) come from the start of a
trace and are unaffected. Collections from `f38a2828` on (C2) capture in immediate mode.

`scripts/evaluate_c1_tail_robustness.py` reruns paper 1's numbers with every connection ending at
its last data packet, so no feature depends on whether a close was written
(`data/results/c1_tail_robustness.{json,md}`).

## TCP retransmissions

A retransmitted TCP segment repeats bytes an observer has already seen. C1 (loopback) has 398, all
24-byte TLS close alerts an agent sent again about 100 ms later while its connection closed, each
after its connection's last data packet; the released C1 features count them
(`data/results/c1_dedup_robustness.{json,md}` reruns paper 1's numbers without them). The C2 pilot
on the home Wi-Fi had 3 to 12 retransmitted segments from the Pi per arm, all spurious (the Mac had
already acknowledged the first copy). From Nov 11, 2026, `a2a_leakcheck`,
`scripts/evaluate_leakcheck.py` and the padding check in `scripts/check_session_design.py` count
every payload byte once (`features/kfp.py`, `dedup`), as an observer that reassembles the TCP
stream does; `scripts/extract_features.py --dedup` does the same for any collection.

## Collection C2 (paper 2): size set after the pilot

On Nov 11, 2026, after the 1-hour pilot (`data/raw_c2_pilot`, `data/raw_c2_pad_pilot`,
`data/raw_c2_padmax_pilot`: 173 tasks per arm, not part of C2), C2 was set to 1,000 tasks per arm
(250 of each task type: alert_triage, sensor_analysis, fault_diagnosis, procedure_lookup) in all
three arms (`lan`, `lan_pad`, `lan_padmax`), seed offset 30000. The decision rested on the pilot's
pace alone (7.1 s per trace, so the 3,000 traces take about 6 hours) and was made before any leakage
result was computed from C2 or from the pilot.

## C2's emulated WAN: dnctl does not show pipe 2 (it was never removed)

`scripts/wan_emulation.sh` delays the Pi's packets to the cloud alias in dummynet pipe 1 (as they
enter the Mac) and the alias's packets to the Pi in pipe 2 (as they leave it), 6 ms each. After the
pilot, `sudo dnctl pipe show` (Nov 11, 21:53) listed only pipe 1 (66,044 packets, 0 drops), and on
Nov 12 `dnctl list` again showed only pipe 1 while `dnctl pipe show 2` printed nothing; after
`wan_emulation.sh off` and `on` (07:48) both were listed. Pipe 2 was there all along; dnctl does not
print it. In a 64-bit process the kernel copies each flow queue out as a 176-byte
`struct dn_flow_queue_64` (xnu `bsd/netinet/ip_dummynet.c`, `dn_copy_set_64`), but dnctl steps over
it as its own 184-byte `struct dn_flow_queue` (`network_cmds` `dnctl.c`, `list_pipes`). Once pipe 1
has a queue, which its first packet creates, dnctl lands 8 bytes into pipe 2's record, finds no pipe
marker and stops; with no traffic yet, as right after `on`, it lists both. Compiling xnu's header
and walking a buffer laid out as the kernel writes it reproduces this. Evidence that pipe 2 delayed
the alias's packets throughout: all 393 Pi → alias handshakes in the pilot's three arms take 11.6 ms
or more from SYN to SYN-ACK at the capture (median 13.2 ms: both pipes), and the WAN check at the
pilot's end (21:36) passed. Between 21:36 and 21:53 nothing configured dummynet; the only commands
were the alias fix (`ifconfig`), which does not touch dummynet pipes. The pilot's captures also show
no gap in the sequence numbers of the 62,917 data segments the Mac sent the Pi; the capture sits
after pipe 2, so pipe 2 dropped none of them.

Read from the kernel on Nov 12 at 21:39 (`scripts/wan_watch.py`, below), with the same request dnctl
makes: pipe 1 (Pi → alias) and pipe 2 (alias → Pi), each at 6 ms with one queue, 360 packets and 0
drops, the traffic of the WAN check at 07:54. In the same sample, `dnctl list` printed only `00001:`.
The kernel returned 1,072 bytes. The two pipe records and their queues end at byte 1,056; the last 16
bytes are zeros, 8 per queue: the kernel sizes the buffer with its 184-byte queue struct but writes
176-byte records. The first rows of `data/raw_c2/wan_watch.jsonl` (21:35 to 21:39) carry a reader
error instead: that version asked Python's `socket.getsockopt` for a 1 MB buffer, which Python refuses
above 1,024 bytes. The reader now calls libc's `getsockopt`.

From Nov 12, `scripts/wan_watch.py` (run with sudo) reads the pipes from the kernel with the
kernel's own record sizes, plus the pf anchor, every 30 s (`data/raw_c2/wan_watch.jsonl`,
`data/raw_c2/wan_status.json`); `scripts/c2_guard.py` checks C2 against it. At the start and every
50 traces, it checks four things:
- both pipes at 6 ms, with no bandwidth limit or loss rate;
- pf is enabled and the anchor's two dummynet rules are loaded;
- the port lock;
- in the traces since the last check, the Pi's connect time to the alias, 22.6 ms ±20%.

The capture shows that connect time as SYN to the Pi's ACK, which is 3.3 ms shorter than the Pi's
own timer reads it. That offset was measured on the 90 probes of the WAN check at 07:54 (Pi 20.22 ms,
capture 16.91 ms, `data/c2_wan_calibration/wan_check_probe.pcap`; it is kept out of `data/raw_c2`,
where the collector refuses to start over existing pcaps: the first start, at 21:40, stopped there
with no traces, and the checks it then ran over the empty folders are in `data/_c2_aborted_2140`).
The guard also requires the capture's SYN to SYN-ACK to be 12 ms ±20%, with no handshake under
9.6 ms (a missing pipe halves it). If a check fails, it stops the collection and lists the traces
since the last good check as suspect (`data/raw_c2/wan_guard.json`, with each pipe's drop counter at
every check).

## Verify, then unpack

From the repository root, **verify the download integrity first** (the release ships a
`SHA256SUMS.txt` alongside the zips; the feature checksum is also pasted above):

```bash
# verify (matches the checksum in the release / above)
shasum -a 256 a2a-features.zip        # compare against the SHA-256 above
#   or, against the shipped manifest:  shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS.txt

# features (enough for the short demo)
unzip a2a-features.zip -d .        # creates data/processed*/...

# raw captures (needed for the full suite)
unzip a2a-raw-pcaps.zip -d .       # creates data/raw*/...
```

## Re-packaging (maintainer)

To rebuild the archives from a populated `data/` directory:

```bash
bash scripts/package_data.sh   # writes dist/a2a-features.zip, dist/a2a-raw-pcaps.zip, dist/SHA256SUMS.txt
```

Upload the two zips **and `dist/SHA256SUMS.txt`** to the data host, then paste the DOI/URL
into the **Archive location** line above and both SHA-256 values into the **SHA-256** lines,
so downloaders can verify. This file is the only place they are recorded.

Once the TLS control has been collected (`scripts/run_tls_control.sh`), the packager adds
its four sets automatically (`processed_tlspair_clear`, `processed_tlspair_tls`,
`raw_tlspair_clear`, `raw_tlspair_tls`), making 19 feature sets and 24 raw sets; rebuild and
upload after that run so the checksums match.

## Provenance & ethics

- tcpdump ran with a 96-byte snaplen and a BPF filter scoped to the agent host:port
  flows. That snaplen still kept a 20-40 byte slice of cleartext payload per packet,
  so before release every packet is cut to its link, IP and TCP headers
  (`scripts/strip_payload.py`, run by `scripts/package_data.sh`). Timestamps and
  on-wire lengths are preserved, and features re-extracted from the stripped
  captures are identical to the published ones.
- All traffic was generated on infrastructure owned by the researchers; no
  external systems were targeted.
- Raw collection is stochastic (LLM generation over a live network), so a fresh
  collection produces a *different* dataset with point estimates inside the
  published CIs — it does not reproduce the exact numbers. Deterministic
  reproduction is of the **analysis** from the frozen features, via
  `scripts/reproduce.sh`.
